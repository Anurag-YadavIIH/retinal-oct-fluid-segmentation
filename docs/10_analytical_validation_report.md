## Summary

**The pre-registered question** (`docs/07` §17.1 and §17.7(c); the leave-one-vendor-out design
is described in `docs/06` §6): when a fluid-segmentation model is trained on two OCT vendors, how
much worse does it perform on the third, unseen vendor? The comparison is against unseen patients
from the vendors it was trained on.

**Headline — primary, pre-registered.** By the pre-registered criterion, the overlap of the two
arms' 95% intervals, **one of nine comparisons establishes a difference in Dice: spectralis SRF,
where the unseen vendor scores higher**, 0.6088 [0.4587, 0.7519] n=24, against 0.1268 [0.0000, 0.3779] n=7 for unseen patients of the training vendors. Beside it:

- the in-domain arm is 71% SRF-absent (5 of 7 volumes), and all 5 of those volumes scored Dice
  0, because the model marks some SRF where there is none (the false-positive pattern of §7.1);
  on the held-out side, 7 of the 10 SRF-absent volumes scored Dice 1;
- restricted to volumes containing SRF, the same comparison's difference interval includes
  zero: +0.0999 [−0.4211, +0.6199];
- no multiplicity correction was pre-registered for the nine comparisons.

The difference is consistent with case mix; this report does not claim that case mix explains
it. **No degradation on an unseen vendor is established by the pre-registered criterion**, in
any fold or class. The other eight comparisons establish no difference in either direction.

**Post-hoc, restricted to volumes containing the fluid.** Bootstrapping the held-out −
in-domain difference directly and pooling over folds, the IRF and PED intervals exclude zero
with the held-out vendor lower: IRF −0.1268 [−0.2030, −0.0463] (55 held-out against 11
in-domain patients), PED −0.1571 [−0.2474, −0.0617] (33 against 3). Of all 24 difference
intervals, 7 exclude zero, and 6 of those 7 have the held-out vendor lower. They are
uncorrected, chosen after the results were visible, and PED's in-domain arm is 3 patients:
**suggestive, not established.**

**The primary and class-present views point in opposite directions on the same data, because
of the empty-scan convention.** A volume without a fluid type scores Dice 1 if nothing is
predicted and 0 if anything is (§5.2). The primary, all-volume view includes those volumes. The
arms differ both in how many such volumes they hold and in how often the model predicts nothing
on them, which can raise one arm and lower the other. The class-present view removes those
volumes and compares segmentation only where there is fluid to segment. Neither view corrects
the other: the first is the pre-registered result, the second is post-hoc.

**What the study could detect** (post-hoc, §5.4, derived from directly bootstrapped difference
intervals). An observed difference must exceed half the interval's width to exclude zero:

- **per fold:** 0.18–0.32 Dice;
- **pooled across folds:** 0.14–0.20 Dice.

A true difference of that size would be detected only about half the time. **Differences
smaller than about 0.14 Dice cannot be detected by this design**, and per-fold differences
below about 0.3 are detected unreliably. **The main limitation is the in-domain arms: 7
patients per fold**, which leaves their Dice intervals 0.21–0.52 wide.

**The design removed the most obvious vendor signature on purpose.** Per-volume intensity
normalisation took out the Topcon brightness offset, a detector pedestal perfectly correlated
with vendor. So the absence of a detectable gap may partly reflect that choice, and every gap
here is a lower bound on the raw-data gap (§8.1).

**Further findings, in order of weight.**

1. **False positives in class-absent volumes dominate the all-volume Dice** (§7.1; secondary,
   pre-registered except for cirrus, where it is post-hoc). In 14 of 18 fold × arm × class
   cells, every class-absent volume scored Dice 0: the model predicted some of the class where
   there was none. This also **confounds vendor with case mix** (§5.2, post-hoc). The
   in-domain arms carry more class-absent volumes than the held-out arms, 62% against 47% for
   SRF and 77% against 53% for PED, pooled, so their all-volume Dice is pulled down harder.
2. **The metric is fragile in small arms** (§7.2). One model's in-domain SRF Dice moved by 0.1431
   between two equally valid MC-dropout draws, both measured through the defect. One
   class-absent volume flipping between Dice 1 and 0 moves a 7-patient mean by 1/7 = 0.1429. The
   record that would confirm this mechanism does not exist.
3. **The pooled comparison** (§5.1–§5.3; post-hoc pooling rule). Pooled over folds with the
   patient as the unit (70 held-out against 13 in-domain), all three classes overlap on all
   volumes. On class-present volumes alone, IRF and SRF overlap and PED does not, with the
   held-out vendor lower and an in-domain arm of 3 patients.

**What this report does not claim.** It does not claim that the model generalises to unseen
vendors, nor that it generalises better or worse than in domain. It does not claim the absence
of a vendor effect: the post-hoc class-present view points towards one, and the design cannot
see a difference smaller than its detection limit.

## 1. Objective

Quantify the cross-vendor generalisation of retinal OCT fluid segmentation (IRF, SRF, PED).
Train on two vendors, evaluate on the third, and compare against unseen patients of the
training vendors. Report every figure per class, per vendor, with a patient-level 95% interval
and its `n` (`docs/07` §17.2).

## 4. Results — detection (primary, pre-registered)

**Unchanged by every correction, in every cell.** No volume's predicted voxel count crossed the
10-voxel threshold under the corrected inversion (`docs/08` §5i.2), and under the final
re-evaluation, with the score read from the native content of each frame (D4), sensitivity,
specificity and AUROC are identical to the §17.12 records in all 18 cells (`docs/08` §5k.2).

Sensitivity and specificity are at the fixed threshold of 10 predicted voxels, reported as they
fell (§17.7a). **AUROC is primary, because it is threshold-free.** The pre-registered AUROC
carries **no interval**: the sealed records of 2026-10-05 did not persist the per-volume score
(`docs/08` D9), which is a gap against `docs/07` §17.2 (§8.7).

**The AUROC intervals in the table are post-hoc.** They were computed on 2026-10-24, after every
result was visible, from the per-volume scores the corrected records persist (SRS-089 as
amended), and they are not part of the pre-registered result. Method: in each cell the
positive and the negative volumes are resampled separately, with replacement, keeping both
counts fixed; 2000 replicates, seed 20260916, scored by the project's own `auroc`
(`metrics.py:221`), percentile interval. Each patient contributes one volume to a cell, so
resampling patients is resampling volumes. Before any interval was drawn, the recomputed AUROC
equalled the stored value in all 18 cells. **What they can and cannot say:**

- **Twelve cells have AUROC 1.0000, and their interval is not estimable** by this method
  (note below the table).
- **The in-domain arms hold one to six negatives and one to six positives per class**, and a
  stratified resample of a single negative or positive never varies that side at all.
  Intervals over so few volumes carry little information.
- **Six cells have AUROC below 1**, five of them held-out, and there the intervals are
  informative: lower bounds 0.8472–0.9583 in the held-out arms, and [0.3000, 1.0000] for
  spectralis in-domain SRF (+2/−5).

An unstratified resample, with the replicates that lacked a positive or a negative dropped,
gave intervals within 0.005 of these in the five held-out cells below 1, and 0.2000 rather than
0.3000 as the lower bound for spectralis in-domain SRF (derived, for comparison only; not
reported as a result).

## 5. Generalisation gap

The per-fold gaps are in the tables of §3. This section reports the pooled view and what
confounds it.

### 5.1 Pooled across folds — secondary, **post-hoc pooling rule**

§19.3 pre-registered a pooled comparison. Its own assertion then failed: the three in-domain
arms hold 21 volumes from **13** patients, and 8 patients appear in two folds. The author fixed
the rule after the per-fold results were visible (`docs/07` §19.5, committed `3ed36ae` before
computing):

- **the patient is the unit**;
- an in-domain patient contributes the mean of its Dice across the folds holding it;
- a held-out patient contributes its single value.

### 5.2 All-volume Dice confounds vendor with case mix — post-hoc

**The mechanism.** Under the both-empty convention, a class-absent volume scores Dice 1.0 if
nothing is predicted and 0.0 if anything is (`metrics.py:104-117`). This model nearly always
predicts something (§7.1), so **class-absent volumes score 0**. An arm's all-volume Dice is
therefore pulled down in proportion to its share of class-absent volumes, whatever the vendor.
If two arms differ in that share, their all-volume Dice differs for a reason that has nothing
to do with vendor shift.

### 7.2 Metric fragility in small arms

**Measured.** Cirrus in-domain SRF Dice is 0.2707 in the unseeded draw and 0.1276 in the seeded
one: −0.1431, with the same model, threshold and metric (§3.1). **Arithmetic:** one
class-absent volume flipping between Dice 1 and 0 moves a 7-patient mean by 1/7 = 0.1429. The
arm has 4 SRF-absent volumes. **Not confirmable:** the unseeded record kept no per-volume rows
(`docs/08` D7). The HD95 `n` changing from 4 to 3 is consistent with a single flip, not proof
of it.

**Both draws were measured through the inversion defect.** The corrected seeded figure is 0.1377 (§3). No corrected unseeded draw exists, so the shift between draws is not re-measured.
The single-flip arithmetic does not depend on the inversion, and the §19.1 absent-volume Dice
that drive it were unchanged by the correction (§7.1).

**Why it matters for reading §3.** A shift this large, from sampling alone, is 37% of the width
of the narrowest in-domain Dice interval of the unseeded record (`docs/08` §5g.2a). Every
small-arm Dice here carries that sensitivity. Seeding makes each figure reproducible. It does
not make it less sensitive to which voxels a single draw happens to predict.

## 8. Limitations

### 8.1 The measured cross-vendor gap is a LOWER BOUND on the raw-data gap

**Every reported cross-vendor figure is obtained after per-volume intensity
normalisation** (SRS-070): the 1st and 99th percentiles of each volume are mapped to 0
and 1 and values outside are clipped. This removes the part of the vendor difference
that is a difference in encoding rather than in imaging, so **the generalisation gap in
§5 is smaller than the gap raw data would produce.** It is a lower bound, and no figure
in this document should be read as the degradation a naive pipeline would suffer.

**What specifically is removed.** The clearest case is Topcon, which carries a raised
black level: measured across the training partition, its 1st percentile is **35/255**,
against 0/255 for Cirrus and 0/65535 for Spectralis. That is a detector offset, not
tissue. Under dtype-maximum scaling it survives as a constant brightness offset
**perfectly correlated with vendor** — measured median as a fraction of full scale is
0.122 for Cirrus, 0.132 for Spectralis and 0.235 for Topcon, so two vendors coincide and
the third is separated by a factor of nearly two for a reason that has nothing to do with
retinal anatomy. A network can read vendor identity straight off the brightness, and a
model that does so scores well in domain and has learned nothing that transfers.

**Why removing it is defensible rather than convenient.** Three reasons, and the first is
the one that matters:

1. **Leaving it in would not measure vendor shift, it would measure a bug.** A gap driven
   by an uncorrected detector offset is not evidence about whether segmentation
   transfers across scanners; it is evidence that the preprocessing was wrong. The
   residual gap after normalisation is attributable to speckle, axial resolution,
   sampling density and population differences — the things the question is actually
   about.
2. **Any deployable system would normalise.** Reporting the un-normalised gap would
   describe a system nobody would build.
3. **The correction uses no information the held-out vendor does not carry itself.** The
   window is computed from each volume individually, so the held-out vendor is treated
   identically to the training vendors and nothing about the training distribution
   leaks into test preprocessing.

**What is not claimed.** Normalisation does not remove vendor shift; it removes the
trivially correctable component. Speckle statistics, axial resolution (a factor of two
between Cirrus and Spectralis before resampling), B-scan density (49 against 128) and
field of view all differ and all survive. Those are the shift this project measures.

**How to quantify what was removed.** §5's gap can be compared against a fold trained
with dtype-maximum scaling instead of the percentile window, which would isolate the
pedestal's contribution. That run is **conditional on quota** and is not part of the
primary result; if it was not run, this section stands as a stated bound rather than a
measured one, and says so here rather than leaving the reader to assume either.

**Status, 2026-10-05: the dtype-scaling comparison run was not done.** This section therefore stands as a stated bound, not a measured one, as it said it would. Note also that no degradation is established in §3, and the one established difference favours the unseen vendor. The bound applies to the point differences.

### 8.2 Axial resampling

All frames are resampled axially to a declared 0.004 mm/pixel (SRS-072). The target is
the **coarsest** common choice, so no vendor is ever upsampled: upsampling would
fabricate detail and could substitute a smoothness signature for the brightness one
removed above. Cirrus is downsampled by a factor of about two and loses real axial
detail; that cost is accepted and is measurable by comparison against a native-resolution
run. All metrics and all volumes in mm³ are computed in native geometry (SRS-074), so no
reported number is expressed at the resampled spacing.

**Status, 2026-10-05: the native-resolution comparison run was not done.** The cost of downsampling cirrus is accepted and unmeasured.

### 8.3 The in-domain arms are too small to resolve the question — **the main limitation**

Each fold's in-domain reference holds 7 patients. Its Dice intervals are 0.22–0.52 wide; the widest is topcon SRF, [0.0000, 0.5216]. Its class-present strata hold 1–6 patients. Pooling
raises the in-domain arm only to **13** patients, not the 21 that §19.3 anticipated, because 8
patients sit in two folds' references (§5.1). **Negative findings here are underpowered, not
reassuring.** A larger in-domain reference would need more data than the 70 public volumes
provide (§8.5).

### 8.4 The reference standard is of unquantified variability

Annotations are manual, from two centres, with no published per-case inter-rater agreement and
no double grading (`docs/06` §1.2). Reference variability is therefore an unquantified part of
every Dice and HD95 here. Small structures, PED in class-absent-heavy arms especially, are where
it would weigh most.

### 8.5 Seventy volumes, and no official test partition

Only the RETOUCH training partition carries public annotations, so every fold is built within
70 volumes (`docs/06` §1.3), with held-out arms of 22–24 patients. **The intervals in §3 are
wide because the data is small**, and narrower ones from this dataset would indicate a defect.

### 8.6 The pre-registered pooling assumption was false

§19.3 assumed each patient in at most one fold's in-domain reference "by construction". It was
not, and the pooling rule actually used was chosen after the per-fold results were visible
(§5.1). The pooled figures are post-hoc as a result. Each held-out patient is in exactly one
fold, so the held-out arms are unaffected.

### 8.7 Scope of the reproducibility claim, and the AUROC interval

- **Reproducibility is established on this machine only.** Seeded evaluation reproduced itself
  exactly on this GTX 1050 and torch build. On another card or build it is unproven until
  re-run (HAZ-016 residual).
- **Cirrus also has an unseeded figure, kept on the record.** It is a single draw that cannot
  be reproduced (§3.1).
- **The sealed AUROCs have no intervals, because the detection score was not persisted**
  (`docs/08` D9), against `docs/07` §17.2. A patient-level AUROC bootstrap needs each volume's
  MC-dropout mean probability. The per-volume rows of every sealed record carry Dice, HD95,
  voxel counts and reference presence, but not that score: the pipeline used it only inside
  the aggregate. This is the D7 defect repeated for a second field. **It is repaired for future
  evaluations** (SRS-089 amended, TC-126 extended), not retroactively.
- **No sealed bucket was re-unlocked to recover the scores.** Recovering them would cost one
  more unlock on each of the six buckets, about five hours of GPU, and would raise every
  bucket's access count for one statistic. The author declined, for a reason the records show:
  **the in-domain arms hold 1–6 negatives and 1–6 positives per class**. Three cells have at
  most 2 negatives and five have at most 2 positives. An interval over one negative or two
  positives carries almost no information.
- **The scores exist now, for a different reason.** The §17.11 re-evaluation, run to correct
  the inversion defect, wrote records under the amended SRS-089, which persist each volume's
  score. The AUROC values are unchanged (§4), so §4 now gives **post-hoc** intervals computed
  from those scores. They bear out the reasoning above: the in-domain intervals carry little
  information, and twelve of the eighteen cells, held-out and in-domain, have AUROC 1.0000 and
  an interval that is not estimable by this method. The six informative intervals are the five
  held-out cells below 1, with lower bounds 0.8472–0.9583, and spectralis in-domain SRF,
  [0.3000, 1.0000]. The pre-registered AUROC remains a point value without an interval. The final records persist
the scores too, and the intervals in §4 are computed from them; the AUROC values themselves are
identical to the §17.12 records in every cell.

### 8.8 Image quality is not separated from appearance shift

Cirrus is reported in the literature as lower in image quality (`docs/06` §3.4). Held-out
cirrus Dice is not the lowest of the three folds for any class: topcon is lowest for SRF and
PED, and spectralis for IRF (§3). Through the defect, cirrus was lowest for SRF and PED (§10).
**These are comparisons across three different models, and were never pre-registered.** The
pre-registered comparison is within each fold, and it establishes no degradation (§3). So neither image quality nor appearance shift is credited with any
cross-vendor pattern: none is established for either to explain.

## 9. Conclusion

Under a pre-registered, seeded and reproducible protocol, all three folds were evaluated once,
and every sealed bucket once more after a measuring defect was found and fixed (§2, §10).

**Headline — primary, pre-registered.** By the pre-registered criterion, the overlap of the two
arms' 95% intervals, **one of nine comparisons establishes a difference in Dice: spectralis SRF,
where the unseen vendor scores higher**, 0.6088 [0.4587, 0.7519] n=24, against 0.1268 [0.0000, 0.3779] n=7 for unseen patients of the training vendors. Beside it:

- the in-domain arm is 71% SRF-absent (5 of 7 volumes), and all 5 of those volumes scored Dice
  0, because the model marks some SRF where there is none (the false-positive pattern of §7.1);
  on the held-out side, 7 of the 10 SRF-absent volumes scored Dice 1;
- restricted to volumes containing SRF, the same comparison's difference interval includes
  zero: +0.0999 [−0.4211, +0.6199];
- no multiplicity correction was pre-registered for the nine comparisons.

The difference is consistent with case mix; this report does not claim that case mix explains
it. **No degradation on an unseen vendor is established by the pre-registered criterion**, in
any fold or class. The other eight comparisons establish no difference in either direction.

**Post-hoc, restricted to volumes containing the fluid.** Bootstrapping the held-out −
in-domain difference directly and pooling over folds, the IRF and PED intervals exclude zero
with the held-out vendor lower: IRF −0.1268 [−0.2030, −0.0463] (55 held-out against 11
in-domain patients), PED −0.1571 [−0.2474, −0.0617] (33 against 3). Of all 24 difference
intervals, 7 exclude zero, and 6 of those 7 have the held-out vendor lower. They are
uncorrected, chosen after the results were visible, and PED's in-domain arm is 3 patients:
**suggestive, not established.**

**The primary and class-present views point in opposite directions on the same data, because
of the empty-scan convention.** A volume without a fluid type scores Dice 1 if nothing is
predicted and 0 if anything is (§5.2). The primary, all-volume view includes those volumes. The
arms differ both in how many such volumes they hold and in how often the model predicts nothing
on them, which can raise one arm and lower the other. The class-present view removes those
volumes and compares segmentation only where there is fluid to segment. Neither view corrects
the other: the first is the pre-registered result, the second is post-hoc.

The design cannot detect differences below about 0.14 Dice, and detects per-fold differences
below about 0.3 unreliably (§5.4). Per-volume normalisation removed the most obvious vendor
signature by design, so every measured gap is a lower bound on the raw-data gap (§8.1). The
study's main contributions are therefore methodological and diagnostic, not a generalisation
claim:

- the cross-vendor comparison measured to a stated precision;
- all-volume Dice on this data shown to be dominated by false positives in class-absent volumes,
  and confounded with case mix;
- a demonstration that a single MC-dropout draw can move a small-arm Dice by 0.14;
- a measuring defect found after publication, fixed, and re-evaluated under a pre-registered
  amendment, with both sets of figures kept (§10);
- an audit of the whole pipeline after publication that found twelve defects, fixed each with a
  test shown failing first, re-evaluated once more, and found the headline unchanged (§11).

A study that could answer the question needs more in-domain patients than this dataset holds.

## 11. The audit of 2026-10-28

**What was checked.** After this report was published, the author asked for a full audit of the
evaluation, before any further use of it (`docs/13`, 2026-10-28 and 2026-10-29):

- **every segmentation metric of every open `val` volume, recomputed independently**, with its own
  MetaImage reader, Dice from counts and HD95 by a different algorithm, against the pipeline and
  against the stored records (SRS-103): 0 differences over 21 volumes;
- **an oracle round trip** (TC-151): label maps built to survive resampling were pushed through
  the real preparation and inversion and had to come back exactly, and thin bands had to come back
  with no systematic shift;
- the evaluation script, the report generator, the illustration script and the DICOM writers,
  read line by line against their requirements.

**The twelve defects, and what each changed.** Each was fixed with a test shown failing first, and
each has its own `docs/13` entry.

| Finding | Defect | What it changed |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | The inversion gave each native row the resampled row above it, moving every prediction about one native row deeper | Every Dice, HD95 and predicted voxel count, slightly: at most 0.0072 in an arm's Dice, 0.0298 mm in its HD95. **The training half is not repaired**: labels sat on average about one native row off their images during training, the models are not retrained, and retraining would be a new pre-registered experiment |
| D2 | The report bootstrapped a difference with the two arms resampled independently, though the pooled arms share patients | The pooled difference intervals and the detection limit, from about 0.16 to about 0.14 Dice; no verdict |
| D3 | HD95 scores a both-empty volume as 0 mm and averages it in | Nothing pre-registered: a secondary HD95 without those volumes is added beside the primary (§3) |
| D4 | The detection score's maximum ran over the padding as well as the image | Nothing measured: every detection figure identical, 18 of 18 cells |
| D5 | The spacing check compared the manifest's spacing with itself | Nothing measured: the header spacing equals the manifest's in 70 of 70 volumes |
| D6 | The SR's tracking identifiers were not valid UIDs | DICOM output only |
| D7 | The inversion test's tolerance could absorb a shift of a row | A test only: it now cannot |
| D8 | Segmentation intervals ignored the plan's resample count and α | Nothing at the plan in use, 2000 resamples at α 0.05 |
| D9 | Some stated figures were rounded twice; percentages rounded half to even | Printed figures only |
| D10 | The report check could find a statement inside a longer number, and failed on zero or empty results | The check only |
| D11 | SEG segments were numbered by list position, not by the configured label index | DICOM output only |
| D12 | A big-endian source was refused, and the distance between B-scans was not written as such | DICOM output only |

**What the audit added so that this cannot recur.** Every predicted label map of the final run
is saved with a checksum, so a later measurement defect is corrected by re-measuring, without
the model (SRS-106). No sealed bucket opens without a completed dry run of its chain on
synthetic data at the same commit (SRS-107). Every script a chain calls has an end-to-end test of
its real `main`, and a static type check over the code runs in CI (SRS-109).

**The headline held.** By the pre-registered criterion, 1 of 9 Dice comparisons establishes a
difference, spectralis SRF with the unseen vendor higher, and no degradation on an unseen vendor
is established, as before the audit. The post-hoc class-present pooled intervals for IRF and PED
still exclude zero with the held-out vendor lower: suggestive, not established. Detection is
unchanged in every cell.
